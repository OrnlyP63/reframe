Reframing problems means shifting your perspective to redefine an issue, allowing you to ask better questions and uncover innovative solutions. Instead of getting stuck on a surface-level annoyance, you dig into the root cause and context to find the most functional and accurate interpretation. [1, 2, 3] 
## Why You Should Reframe

* Prevents solving the wrong problem: Ensures you are fixing the actual root cause rather than just treating symptoms.
* Unlocks creativity: Opens up a wider "solution space" by challenging assumptions and blind spots.
* Alters emotional impact: Shifts focus from something you suffer from to an objective you can tackle. [1, 3, 4, 5, 6] 

## A Classic Example: The Slow Elevator

* Original Problem: "The elevator is too slow, and tenants are complaining."
* Typical (Expensive) Solutions: Install a new lift or upgrade the motor.
* The Reframe: "The wait is annoying and causes frustration."
* Better Solutions: Put up mirrors in the lobby or play music. Tenants look at themselves or listen to music, and the wait no longer feels agonizing. [7] 

## Step-by-Step Reframing Framework

   1. Identify the stated problem: Write down the exact issue as it appears to you.
   2. Challenge assumptions: Ask, "What constraints am I assuming to be true?" or "What if the opposite were true?"
   3. Change the question: Shift the statement from how to do something to why you are doing it. (e.g., Instead of "How do we force employees to use this new software?" reframe to "Why are employees struggling with this software, and what do they need to be productive?")
   4. Broaden the context: Look outside the immediate scope to see what external variables or perspectives you might be missing. [3, 8] 

## When to Apply It

* Business: Redefining an unhappy customer complaint into an opportunity to improve customer experience. [7, 9] 
* Productivity: Changing "I don't have enough time" to "Time management for this task requires a different prioritization of my schedule."
* Personal: Adopting a different lens—such as changing "I am lonely" to "I am experiencing a period of aloneness where I can rest and focus on myself." [1]
